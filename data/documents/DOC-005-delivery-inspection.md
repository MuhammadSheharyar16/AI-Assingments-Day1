# DOC-005 — Delivery, Logistics and Inspection

**Document ID:** DOC-005
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Logistics and Goods Receipt, Meridian Procurement Ltd
**Classification:** Internal — Synthetic Training Material

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document sets out how goods are delivered to Meridian Procurement Ltd sites, how they are received and inspected, and what happens when goods do not conform to the order.

It applies to every delivery made against a purchase order, including deliveries made by a supplier's own fleet and deliveries made by a nominated carrier such as Brackenfield Logistics Ltd.

## 2. Delivery Windows

Every purchase order states a required delivery date and a delivery window. The standard window is a four hour slot agreed at the point the order is acknowledged.

A delivery arriving outside its agreed window may be refused at the gate where accepting it would disrupt other bookings. A refused delivery is treated as a failed delivery attempt and the supplier bears the cost of the return and redelivery.

Where a supplier knows it cannot meet the agreed window, it must notify goods receipt at least twenty four hours before the window opens and propose a new slot.

Standard receiving hours are 07:00 to 17:00 Monday to Friday. Deliveries outside receiving hours require prior arrangement and are accepted only where the site has confirmed cover.

## 3. Consignment Documentation

Every consignment must be accompanied by a delivery note quoting the purchase order number, the supplier name, the line items, the quantity delivered against each line, and the total number of packages.

A consignment arriving without a delivery note quoting a valid purchase order number is held unbooked and is not treated as delivered. The supplier is contacted for the missing reference.

Where a single order is fulfilled from more than one location, each consignment carries its own delivery note and the notes cross-reference each other.

## 4. Partial Delivery

A partial delivery will only be accepted where it was agreed in advance and recorded against the purchase order, or where the buyer confirms acceptance at the point the consignment arrives.

An unagreed partial delivery may be refused in full. Where it is accepted, the order remains open and the outstanding balance retains its original required delivery date.

The supplier may not invoice for a partial delivery as though the order were complete. Each accepted consignment is invoiced against the quantity actually received and booked in.

Instalment deliveries agreed at the point of order are treated as separate delivery events, each with its own window and its own delivery note.

## 5. Late Delivery

A delivery is late where it arrives after the required delivery date, or where it arrives within the required date but outside the agreed window and is refused.

The supplier must notify goods receipt as soon as it becomes aware that a delivery will be late, state the revised date, and confirm the cause. A notification given after the required date has passed is not treated as advance notice.

Repeated late delivery affects the supplier's delivery performance score, which is one of the criteria used at re-evaluation. Two consecutive review periods below the agreed threshold are a re-evaluation trigger.

Where a late delivery causes the organisation to incur cost that could not reasonably be avoided, that cost is recoverable as a commercial matter under the agreement.

## 6. Packaging and Labelling

Goods must be packaged so that they arrive undamaged under normal handling. Packaging must be appropriate to the mode of transport and the season.

Every package is labelled with the purchase order number, the line reference, the package number and the total package count for that consignment. Hazardous items carry the classification and handling markings required by the applicable regulations.

Returnable packaging is identified on the delivery note. Where returnable packaging is used, the supplier arranges collection within twenty working days.

## 7. Goods Receipt

Goods are booked in against the purchase order at the point of receipt. Booking in records the quantity received, the date and time, the condition on arrival and the name of the receiver.

Booking in is a record of receipt only. It is not acceptance of the goods and does not prevent a later rejection following inspection.

A discrepancy between the delivery note and the quantity physically received is recorded at booking in and notified to the supplier within one working day.

## 8. Inspection

Goods are subject to an inspection window of ten working days from the date of booking in. Inspection checks quantity, specification compliance, visible damage and the presence of required certification.

Where goods are booked in during a shutdown period, the inspection window begins on the first working day after the shutdown ends.

Goods not rejected within the inspection window are treated as accepted, except where a defect could not reasonably have been identified by inspection. A latent defect may be raised at any point within the warranty period stated in the agreement.

## 9. Non-Conforming Goods

Where goods are rejected, the organisation notifies the supplier stating the line affected, the quantity rejected and the reason.

The supplier proposes a remedy within five working days. Acceptable remedies are replacement, repair, or credit. The choice of remedy rests with the organisation where the rejection concerns specification compliance.

Rejected goods are held at the supplier's risk from the date of the rejection notice. The supplier arranges collection within ten working days. Goods not collected within twenty working days may be disposed of at the supplier's cost.

Replacement goods are subject to a fresh inspection window on the same terms.

## 10. Title, Risk and Restricted Content

Risk in the goods passes to the organisation on booking in. Title passes on the earlier of booking in or payment.

Where goods are rejected, risk passes back to the supplier on the date of the rejection notice.

Site access codes, gate booking system credentials, named goods receipt staff and the site security schedule are restricted content and are not published in supplier-facing documentation.

Delivery records, inspection outcomes and rejection notices are retained for seven years from the date of the delivery event.
