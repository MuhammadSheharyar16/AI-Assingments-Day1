# DOC-003 — Pricing, Cost Breakdown and Payment Terms

**Document ID:** DOC-003
**Version:** 1.2
**Owner:** Finance Operations, Meridian Procurement Ltd
**Classification:** Internal — Synthetic Training Material

## 1. Purpose

This document sets out how supplier pricing is structured, how prices may be reviewed during a term, what an acceptable invoice looks like, and when payment is made.

It applies to all agreements held with approved suppliers, including Nordvale Components Ltd, Brackenfield Logistics Ltd, Halvorsen Industrial Supply and Petronella Services Ltd.

## 2. Cost Components

Every quoted price must be broken down into named cost components. A single undifferentiated figure is not accepted at evaluation and is not accepted on an invoice.

The recognised cost components are: unit cost of goods, labour, transport and carriage, tooling and setup, packaging, duties and levies, and any agreed management charge.

Each component is stated separately with its unit of measure and the quantity assumed. Where a component is variable, the quotation must state the basis on which it varies and the maximum exposure over the term.

A cost component that does not appear in the agreed breakdown may not be introduced later through an invoice line. Introducing an unagreed component is a change and must go through change control.

## 3. Rate Cards

Where services are charged by time, a rate card is attached as a schedule to the agreement. The rate card lists each role, the day rate, the standard working day in hours, and any premium applicable to work outside standard hours.

Rates are fixed for the initial term unless the agreement contains an indexation provision. A rate not listed on the card may not be charged.

Travel time is not chargeable unless the agreement states otherwise. Where travel is chargeable, it is charged at fifty percent of the applicable day rate.

Individual supplier rate cards and the comparative rate analysis held by Finance Operations are restricted content and are not shared between suppliers or with requesters.

## 4. Price Review and Indexation

A price review may be requested once in any twelve month period, by either party, and only where the agreement contains an indexation provision.

A request must be submitted at least sixty days before the proposed effective date and must be supported by evidence of the underlying cost movement. An increase is capped at the published index movement for the relevant period.

Where the index has moved downward, the organisation may require a corresponding reduction on the same basis.

An agreed price change is recorded in a signed change note. A price applied without a signed change note is rejected at invoice validation.

## 5. Invoicing Requirements

An invoice is valid only where all of the following are present: the supplier name and registration number, the invoice number and date, the contract reference, the agreed cost components matching the order, the net amount, the tax amount and the total.

A valid purchase order number must appear on every invoice. An invoice without a purchase order number is returned unpaid and is not treated as received for payment purposes.

Invoices are submitted electronically to the accounts payable mailbox. Invoices sent to an operational contact or attached to correspondence are not registered and will not be paid.

One invoice covers one purchase order. Consolidated invoices spanning multiple orders are returned.

## 6. Payment Terms

Standard payment terms are net thirty days from the date of a valid invoice. The clock starts on the date the invoice is registered as valid, not on the date it was issued or sent.

Where an invoice is returned as invalid, the payment clock restarts when a corrected invoice is registered.

Payment is made by bank transfer to the account confirmed during onboarding. A change of bank details requires re-verification before any further payment is released.

## 7. Disputed Invoices

Where part of an invoice is disputed, the undisputed part is paid on the normal terms and the disputed part is held. The organisation notifies the supplier of the disputed amount and the reason within ten working days of registration.

A dispute must be raised before the payment due date. An amount paid in full is not later reclaimed through the dispute process; recovery is handled as a commercial matter.

Withholding supply because an invoice is in dispute is a material breach of the agreement.

## 8. Late Payment

Where the organisation pays a valid invoice after the due date, the supplier may be entitled to claim interest on the overdue amount under the applicable statutory provisions, together with a fixed recovery sum where the statute provides one.

Any such claim must be submitted in writing to Finance Operations with the invoice reference and the number of days overdue. Claims submitted more than six months after the payment date are not considered.

Interest is calculated on the net overdue amount only and does not apply to any portion properly held under the dispute process.

## 9. Early Settlement Discount

Where an agreement contains an early settlement provision, a discount of two percent applies to invoices settled within ten days of registration.

The discount is applied to the net amount before tax. It may not be combined with any other discount or rebate offered under the same agreement.

Early settlement is at the organisation's option and creates no obligation to settle early on any future invoice.

## 10. Currency and Records

All agreements are priced in pounds sterling unless the order form states otherwise. Where an agreement is priced in another currency, the exchange basis and the date of conversion are stated in the schedule.

The supplier bears the cost of any bank charge levied at its own end. The organisation bears the cost of charges levied at its own end.

Payment records, invoice registers and dispute correspondence are retained for seven years from the end of the financial year in which payment was made.
